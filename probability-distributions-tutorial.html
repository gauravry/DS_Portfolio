1) Basic Intuition

ContinuousWhat is Normal?

The normal distribution describes measurements that cluster around a mean (μ) with spread controlled by the standard deviation (σ).

f(x)= (1 / (σ√(2π))) · e^{-(x-μ)²/(2σ²)}

Key features

  • Higher σ → wider curve (more uncertainty).
  • Mean shifts the curve left/right.
  • 68–95–99.7 rule for standard deviations.
  • Symmetric around μ, bell-shaped, and unimodal.
  • Total area under the curve equals 1.
  • Mean = median = mode.

Real-life examples

  • Heights and weights in large populations.
  • Measurement errors from instruments.
  • Standardized test scores and IQ.
  • Daily temperature fluctuations around seasonal averages.
  • Manufacturing tolerances (e.g., bolt diameter).

3) Guided Problems (Basic → Intermediate → Advanced)

Basic

Problem 1: Z-score basics

Problem: Test scores are Normal(70, 10). What is the probability a student scores above 85?

  1. Identify μ = 70 and σ = 10.
  2. Compute z = (85 − 70) / 10.
  3. Find the right-tail probability P(Z > 1.5).
Problem visualization: raw distribution
Solution visualization: shaded tail
Show solution

Compute z = (85 − 70) / 10 = 1.5. Then P(X > 85) = P(Z > 1.5) ≈ 0.0668.

Intermediate

Problem 2: Central 95% interval

Problem: Reaction times are Normal(300 ms, 40 ms). Find the middle 95% range.

  1. Identify μ = 300 and σ = 40.
  2. Use z = 1.96 for the middle 95%.
  3. Compute μ ± 1.96σ to get the interval.
Problem visualization: baseline curve
Solution visualization: central 95%
Show solution

The middle 95% is μ ± 1.96σ. So 300 ± 1.96×40 = 300 ± 78.4 → [221.6, 378.4] ms.

Advanced

Problem 3: Hypothesis test on a mean

Problem: A machine claims μ = 50. A sample of n=36 has mean 48.8. Assume σ = 3. Should we reject at α = 0.05?

  1. State H₀: μ = 50 and H₁: μ ≠ 50.
  2. Compute z = (48.8 − 50) / (3/√36).
  3. Compare |z| to 1.96 or compute the p-value.
  4. Reject H₀ if |z| > 1.96.
Problem visualization: null distribution
Solution visualization: critical regions
Show solution

Test statistic: z = (48.8 − 50) / (3/√36) = -2.4. Since |z| > 1.96, reject H₀ at 5%.

4) Monty Hall Simulation (Probability in Action)

Why it matters

Monty Hall demonstrates conditional probability and why switching choices can improve your odds.

P(win by switching) = 2/3, P(win by staying) = 1/3

Key idea: the host reveals a goat knowing where the car is, so the remaining unopened door carries the combined probability.

Mathematical view

  • Initial pick is correct with probability 1/3.
  • Initial pick is wrong with probability 2/3.
  • Switching wins whenever the initial pick is wrong → 2/3.
